Autism Spectrum Condition is a naturally occurring neurodevelopmental variation. It affects how an individual processes sensory information, communicates, interacts, and understands social dynamics. Modern developmental science recognizes it as a spectrum of differences rather than an illness to be cured, emphasizing acceptance, accommodation, and individualized support.

No. Autism is a multi-dimensional spectrum. Each individual has a distinct combination of communication abilities, sensory sensitivities, cognitive strengths, and support needs. Two individuals on the autism spectrum may have completely different profiles, personalities, and life goals.

Seeking advice is recommended whenever you notice persistent developmental differences in your child—such as delayed speech or communication difficulties, extreme distress in reaction to routine changes, sensory hypersensitivity, social withdrawal, or repetitive behaviors that cause distress. Early guidance provides parents with clear coping tools and supportive strategies.
